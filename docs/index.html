Office Simulation

A living pixel office in your VS Code sidebar. Your guy works at his station while critters scurry past, visitors drop in, a band sets up, and the sun goes down by your real clock. Other extensions can hook in and give their work a worker of its own.

Examples

Every room on this page is the real extension code, the same bundle VS Code loads, driven by the same messages. Click around.

Scenes

Thirteen rooms, each with its own floor, walls, props, critters, and fliers. Outdoor ones get the real sky.

Workers

Each agent an extension sends in gets a guy and a desk. Busy types. Idle and asleep (a suspended session) act the same: they wander, chat, play, and now and then take a nap at the desk. A new guy waits while the maintenance crew carries his desk in and builds it. Add or remove several at once and more of the crew come in to keep up. Click a guy: here that cycles his status.

Your guy and scene events

You have a two-monitor station in the middle of the room. ERROR, HACK, and DEFENSE are short scripted moments: he reacts, his monitors play along, and the room chimes in. Each play picks a different variant.

Activities

The crew carries things to do onto the empty desk spots: pinball, arcade cabinets, ping pong, foosball, a punching bag, yoga mats, a trampoline, and more. Idle guys go use them. Some go alone, some bring a buddy to take turns, and the table games need two. Every few minutes the crew swaps one out for something new: officeSim.activities sets how many there are, and officeSim.activitySwaps (never, rare, normal, often) how often they change. This one swaps often. Add guys and watch the crew clear a spot for each new desk. Take guys away and more activities come in.

Shows

Performers walk in, pick the most open floor as a stage, and the idle guys gather to watch, cheer, and eat. Busy guys keep working and get lunch left on their desks.

Invasions

Now and then something comes through and trashes the place. The lights flicker, everyone freezes, and then they're in: aliens beam down and zap guys (or float one up in a beam), zombies shamble in and anyone bitten gets up green and joins the chase, raiders burst through the door, bonk heads, and smash things, vampires appear in smoke, tackle a guy, and SLURP!, leaving him pale, robots march in and laser guys from across the room, ghosts drift in see-through and one BOO! makes a guy faint, werewolves come with a full moon and anyone bitten runs with the pack, slimes ooze up and SPLAT! guys green and sticky, mummies shuffle in and wrap guys up, and demons open a hellgate and throw fireballs that leave guys sooty. Everyone runs, busy or not. Some hide. Nobody's hurt: once they go, the guys come to one by one, look around, say how weird that was, dust off, sweep up the mess, and get back to it. officeSim.invasions sets how often (off, rare, normal, often).

Visitors

People from other departments drop by: some say a line and leave, some stay to chat, loom over busy desks, or get coffee. Each guy answers in his own personality.

Critters and fliers

Mice, foxes, ducks, bats, little aliens: a troupe runs across every 10–25 seconds. Something flies over every 25–60. Idle guys stop to look.

Chatter

How lines show: every line in a bubble, a few bubbles and the rest as pips, a chat log above the room, or pips only. Bubbles can be solid, soft, or faint. Hover a guy to read his line.

Day and night, and races

Outdoor scenes follow the sun for your clock and the moon's real phase. Guys come in 36 races, each as likely as human.

How it works

Two halves talk over messages: a small extension side that reads settings and the API, and a webview that runs and draws the whole simulation. Nearly all of the logic is pure TypeScript with no VS Code or DOM in it, so it's unit tested and runs the same on this page.

Extension sideReads officeSim.* settings, registers the view and commands, and exposes the API other extensions call.
office · sceneEvent · tint · performance · invasion · visitor · say →← ready · click · showStart · showEnd · invasionStart · invasionEnd · eventEnd
WebviewSizes the room to fill the view, steps the sim each frame, and draws it back to front at a whole-pixel zoom. Pauses while hidden.

The room

officeLayout builds a floor plan for a size in tiles: the back wall and door, desk spots in pairs with seats below, and props along the bottom, per scene. officeShape picks pairs per row and rows of desks for the biggest zoom the view allows, with room for ten desks at least, or the workers plus two. When you're in, the pair nearest the middle joins into your station. Paths are breadth-first searches that don't cut across seats.

The room fills the view at any size. The zoom is the biggest that still fits the desks the workers need. At that zoom the room takes every whole tile the view has, so a wider or taller view gets more floor and more desk spots (which activities fill), with the desks centered and whole pairs only. The last part of a tile at the sides and bottom is drawn as more of the same floor and wall, lit like the rest at night, so there's never a blank band. When a resize only adds or drops tiles around the desks, everyone moves with them: the guys at their desks keep typing. Try it in the simulator: set View to Drag to resize and pull the corner.

The guys

officeSim runs everyone. A busy worker types at his desk. An idle one sits back, then goes to a prop, the couch, a walk, or a chat with another idle guy, and runs back when he's busy again. Asleep (a suspended session) is treated as idle: a nap at the desk is just one more idle thing, picked now and then for half a minute to a minute or so, and a few are napping when the view opens. Going between idle and asleep changes nothing he is doing; going busy wakes him and runs him back. A worker that goes away walks out the door. Desks are real objects: when one is missing, two maintenance guys fetch it from the door, carry it to its spot (the partner brings the chair), and build it, and the new guy waits beside the spot meanwhile. A desk with no worker gets taken apart and hauled out. While desks are still waiting, another maintenance guy comes in every moment or so, up to six, so a batch of new or ended sessions gets one job each. When the room grows or shrinks, desks already standing stay, and the crew still brings in or hauls out the rest.

Empty desk spots get activities (officeActivities). How many depends on the free spots and officeSim.activities, so a big room with few guys gets a lot of them and a full room gets none. Every so often the crew carries one in over his head (a little picture of the thing itself) and sets it down, nothing else showing at its spot meanwhile, or takes one that has stood its time back out. How long that is comes from officeSim.activitySwaps: never (they stay until a guy needs the spot), rare (6 to 12 minutes), normal (2 to 5), or often (under 2, with the crew in and out all the time). An activity goes on one desk spot or a pair of them. A machine like pinball stands on the desk tile, with its player on the seat tile below. A mat or a chair lies on the seat tile. A table spans two desk tiles, with a player at each end. New guys get spots without activities first. When a guy needs a spot that has one, the crew takes it out before his desk comes in. An idle guy picks an activity like he picks the cooler. Solo ones he uses alone. At a turns one, a buddy waits beside him and they swap every ten seconds or so. A versus game needs two, so he brings someone, and the winner and loser each say so at the end. When the room changes shape, activities stay put if their spot is still free, and move if it isn't.

Looks come from a seed. pixelGuy hashes it into skin, hair, clothes, and an accessory, and a separate hash stream picks one of 36 races from pixelRaces, so re-rolling the race keeps his clothes. Each guy is drawn into outlined 12×18 frames per pose and facing, and cached.

Your guy

You get the station in the middle with two monitors. Between events you take breaks: wander, chat, or go look over a busy guy's shoulder. officeYou scripts each moment of an ERROR, HACK, or DEFENSE event (your pose, line, main monitor, and shake), and the room's one-word reactions. A tint message recolors your second monitor's graph with a sweep.

The Office view's title bar has its buttons in this order: Trigger Event, Stop, Quiet, Day / Night, Switch Scene, Chatter, Customize Your Guy, and Settings. Stop ends whatever's playing: the show goes home, an invasion snaps back (everyone up, the mess gone), visitors say bye, and a scene event ends. Quiet (officeSim.quiet, the bell) keeps shows, visitors, invasions, and scene events from coming on their own, in the Office only (a host's own views keep theirs), while Trigger Event still starts any of them. Day / Night makes it night when it's day, or day when it's night, for ten minutes, in every open Office at once; press it again for the clock. Settings opens Settings on officeSim. Trigger Event (officeTriggers) starts anything the Office can play, from one list: the scene events, the invasions, the shows, and a visitor from any department, or Surprise Me for any of them. A keybinding can pass an id like "invasion:zombies". A scene event picked there goes to the host when one has taken over events (onDidRequestEvent), so Overseer plays it in its own views at the same moment.

On its own, the Office plays an event every few minutes (officeSim.events) and moves the tint every 20 to 40 seconds. An extension that plays events or sets the tint itself takes over, so Overseer's views and the Office play the same event at the same moment.

Visitors and shows

officeVisitors sends people from other departments in on the frequency setting, up to five at once. Some say a line and go, some stay a minute or two, some settle in. Bosses wear ties. Every guy has a personality from his seed (suck-up, grumbler, deadpan, snark, eager) that shapes how he answers a boss or anyone else. officePerformances plans shows: who performs, what they wear, their lines and crowd cues, the closer, and the snacks. The sim walks performers to the stage with the most open floor in front, gathers the free guys as a crowd, and sends everyone back after the bows.

Invasions

officeInvasions holds the ten kinds: how they come in and go out (the door, a beam, or a puff of smoke, and out as bats or fading away), how they strike (a bite that turns a guy into one of them, a slurp, a zap from a few tiles off drawn as a bolt, a laser, or a fireball, a bonk, or a spook), the mark a hit leaves (pale, wrapped, slimed, or sooty, until he dusts off), whether they shamble, loot, darken the room, or run from the sunrise, how fast they are, how many come for the room's size, the words that pop off a hit, the mess they leave, and everyone's lines. The sim runs it in four parts. In the warning, the room's tint flickers on, a banner goes up, and everyone (busy guys and you too, but not the crew, who've seen worse) drops what he's doing and freezes. In the attack, the invaders come in one by one and chase the nearest guy still standing that nobody else is after. The guys run for open floor far from them, or hide where they are. A guy who's caught struggles until the strike lands, then goes down seeing stars. A zombie's or werewolf's bite gets him up again as one of them, chasing the rest. An alien's zap sometimes floats him up in a beam instead. A vampire's tackle is followed by a slurp over him, and he's pale after. Now and then an invader smashes a desk or a prop and leaves a mess. The attack ends when everyone's down, or after 30 to 45 seconds. In the retreat, the invaders say a line and go: the aliens beam up, the vampires flee the sunrise as bats, ghosts fade away, slimes and demons vanish in a puff, the zombies, mummies, robots, and werewolves go out the door, and the raiders run off with whatever they grabbed. Anyone still caught goes down, guys in a beam drop, and turned guys keel over. In the aftermath, the guys who were never caught come out of it first, and the rest come to one at a time. Each looks around, says something about what hit him (in his own personality), dusts off, and sweeps up the nearest mess nobody's on. Then he goes back to what he was doing, and busy guys head to their desks. A status change in the middle of it waits until he's back to normal. Guys who walk in during the attack get caught up in it too. An invasion cuts a show short, and no shows or visitors come while one is on.

The world

officeCritters sends a troupe along a clear row from one edge to the other; officeFliers sends a lone flier, a V, a line, or a swarm overhead. Some keep day or night hours, some glow, trail sparks, or cast a shadow. officeDaylight works out the sun's elevation from your clock and a guessed latitude, the moon's real phase, and per scene the sky, stars, and room tint. The webview multiplies the room by a light map that keeps open sky bright and adds pools of light around screens, lamps, and fires.

Talking

officePhrases holds what they say: small talk and scene lines, exchanges the other guy answers, mutters while typing, and musings while idle. officeChatter decides what shows: in tidy, a few bubbles at once by room width, ranked so your lines and visitors go first, and a shown bubble keeps its slot until its line ends. Lines that must be seen always get a bubble. Bubbles are lifted out of each other's way, so they never overlap.

Hooks

Other extensions drive the Office through the API its activate() returns. Without any of them, the room runs with just your guy. Overseer uses these to give every Claude session a worker, play its scene events, and open a session when you click his guy.

import type { OfficeSimulationApi } from "./officeSimulationApi"; // copy api/officeSimulationApi.d.ts from this repo

const office = vscode.extensions.getExtension<OfficeSimulationApi>("djaunt.office-simulation");
const api = office === undefined ? undefined : office.isActive ? office.exports : await office.activate();
if (api?.apiVersion === 1) {
  api.setAgents([{ id: "build", status: "busy", title: "Running the build" }]);
  api.onDidClickAgent((id) => vscode.window.showInformationMessage(`Clicked ${id}`));
}

Treat the Office as optional: check for it, and carry on without it. Every call is checked on the way in, so a bad value is ignored rather than thrown on. Version 1 only grows; anything that would break a host changes apiVersion.

Into the Office

CallWhat it does
setAgents(agents)The workers besides your guy. Each { id, status: "busy" | "idle" | "asleep", title?, seed?, look?, highlighted? } gets a desk; send the whole list each time. Dropping one walks him out. seed picks his look, desk, and personality (it defaults to the id); look is a full custom look over it; highlighted puts a crown on him.
setMainGuy(status)"busy" runs your guy back to his station and keeps him there; "idle" keeps him on a break; "auto" (the default) works with a break now and then.
playEvent(kind, variant?)Plays ERROR, HACK, or DEFENSE in every open Office. The first call stops the Office's own event schedule, so your extension decides when they play. The variant picks the lines; pass the same one to your own views to keep them in step.
setTint(index, burst?)Moves your guy's second monitor graph to a tint in the shared spectrum, with a sweep unless burst is false. The first call stops the Office's own tint cycle.
takeOverEvents()Stops the Office's own event schedule and tint cycle now, for a host that plays its own. Call it when you hook in, so the two never double up.
playShow(kind?)Puts on a show now (, or "random"), opening the Office first if it's hidden.
startInvasion(kind?)Starts an invasion now (, or "random"), opening the Office first if it's hidden. Nothing happens while one is on.
sendVisitor(kind?)Sends in someone from a department (), or a random one.
say(id, text, seconds?)Puts a line over an agent's guy, or yours with "you". Up to 80 characters for 1 to 20 seconds; it goes ahead of the room's own chatter.

Looks

CallWhat it does
lookFromSeed(seed)The look a seed gives, as the Office draws it: a starting point for a custom look.
races(), seedWithRace(seed, race), raceOfSeed(seed)For a race picker: changing a seed's race keeps the rest of his look.
editLook(initial, title?)Opens the character maker over the Office on a look and resolves to what the person saved, or undefined if they cancel. Save it yourself and send it back as an agent's look.

Hosting

attachView(webviewView) draws the Office in a webview view your extension contributes, so it can sit in your own sidebar. Call it from your provider's resolveWebviewView. While a hosted view is attached, the Office's own view hides (the officeSim.hosted context key).

To show the Office on a web page, put embed.html in an iframe: a room that runs by itself, with a few workers coming and going. Add #scene=forest (or any scene id) for another room.

<iframe src="https://tory37.github.io/vscode-office-simulation/embed.html#scene=office" width="560" height="300"></iframe>

Out of the Office

EventFires when
onDidClickAgentA guy is clicked: his agent id, or "you".
onDidStartShow, onDidEndShowA show starts or ends, with its kind.
onDidStartInvasion, onDidEndInvasionAn invasion starts, or ends with everyone back on his feet and the mess swept up, with its kind.
onDidFinishEventA scene event finishes playing, with its kind.
onDidRequestEventSomeone asked for a scene event in the Office (its Trigger Event button) after your extension took over events. Play it with playEvent and anywhere else you show events, so they play together. Until something listens, a request plays in the Office alone.
onDidChangeYourLookYour guy's look changes, from the maker or the setting.
onDidOpenViewAn Office view starts, yours or its own.

Under the API

The extension and the webview talk in messages, which is also how every room on this page runs. In: office (the whole state: scene, agents, your look and status, and every setting), sceneEvent, tint, performance, visitor, say, and maker. Out: ready, click, showStart, showEnd, eventEnd, and makerDone. The API is the supported way in; the messages can change.

Character maker

Design a guy: race, skin, hair, eyes, a beard or glasses or a cap, clothes, and the race's own marks, with a preview that turns and walks through his poses. A race only offers what fits it: no cap over horns, no beard on a beak.

In VS Code it opens the first time the Office does, then from Office: Customize Your Guy… or by clicking your guy, and saves to officeSim.yourLook. Hosts open it with editLook: Overseer uses it for Customize Guy on a session.

Claude Code

Without any other extension, the Office can follow Claude Code itself. Run Office: Connect Claude Code…: after you confirm, it adds a few hooks to ~/.claude/settings.json (and keeps a backup of the file the first time). Your guy works while Claude does, and each Claude session in this window's folders gets a worker of his own (officeSim.claude.workers).

Each hook appends Claude's hook input to ~/.claude/office-simulation/events.jsonl and prints nothing, so the agent never sees it and nothing slows down. Office: Disconnect Claude Code takes out exactly those hooks and leaves yours. If an extension like Overseer drives the Office, it takes over and the bridge stands aside.

HookIn the Office
SessionStartA worker walks in and waits for a desk.
UserPromptSubmitHe gets busy, and your guy runs back to his station. His first prompt is his title.
NotificationHe stops and says "need you!"
StopHe says "done!" and goes idle. Your guy takes his breaks again once nobody's busy.
SessionEndHe walks out, and his desk gets hauled away.

Contributing

This page is the documentation. A pull request to main that changes what the Office does, a hook, a setting, a command, a scene, a show, or a look must update docs/index.html in the same PR, with a live example where one fits. A check on every PR enforces it; a pure refactor can carry the no-docs-needed label instead.

npm install
npm test            # unit tests
npm run typecheck
npm run site        # build, then copy the bundle and data the site runs into docs/
npx serve docs      # or any static server, then open the page
