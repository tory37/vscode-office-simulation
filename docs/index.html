Office Simulation

A living pixel office in your VS Code sidebar. Your guy works at his station while critters scurry past, visitors drop in, a band sets up, and the sun goes down by your real clock. Other extensions can hook in and give their work a worker of its own.

Examples

Every room on this page is the real extension code, the same bundle VS Code loads, driven by the same messages. Click around.

Scenes

Thirteen rooms, each with its own floor, walls, props, critters, and fliers. Outdoor ones get the real sky.

Workers

Each agent an extension sends in gets a guy and a desk. Busy types, idle wanders, asleep naps at his desk. A new guy waits while two maintenance guys carry his desk in and build it. Click a guy: here that cycles his status.

Your guy and scene events

You have a two-monitor station in the middle of the room. ERROR, HACK, and DEFENSE are short scripted moments: he reacts, his monitors play along, and the room chimes in. Each play picks a different variant.

Shows

Performers walk in, pick the most open floor as a stage, and the idle guys gather to watch, cheer, and eat. Busy guys keep working and get lunch left on their desks.

Visitors

People from other departments drop by: some say a line and leave, some stay to chat, loom over busy desks, or get coffee. Each guy answers in his own personality.

Critters and fliers

Mice, foxes, ducks, bats, little aliens: a troupe runs across every 10–25 seconds. Something flies over every 25–60. Idle guys stop to look.

Chatter

How lines show: every line in a bubble, a few bubbles and the rest as pips, a chat log above the room, or pips only. Bubbles can be solid, soft, or faint. Hover a guy to read his line.

Day and night, and races

Outdoor scenes follow the sun for your clock and the moon's real phase. Guys come in 36 races, each as likely as human.

How it works

Two halves talk over messages: a small extension side that reads settings and the API, and a webview that runs and draws the whole simulation. Nearly all of the logic is pure TypeScript with no VS Code or DOM in it, so it's unit tested and runs the same on this page.

Extension sideReads officeSim.* settings, registers the view and commands, and exposes the API other extensions call.
office · sceneEvent · tint · performance →← ready · open
WebviewSizes the room, steps the sim each frame, and draws it back to front at a whole-pixel zoom. Pauses while hidden.

The room

officeLayout builds a floor plan for a size in tiles: the back wall and door, desk spots in pairs with seats below, and props along the bottom, per scene. officeShape picks pairs per row and rows of desks for the biggest zoom the view allows, with room for ten desks at least, or the workers plus two. When you're in, the pair nearest the middle joins into your station. Paths are breadth-first searches that don't cut across seats.

The guys

officeSim runs everyone. A busy worker types at his desk. An idle one sits back, then goes to a prop, the couch, a walk, or a chat with another idle guy, and runs back when he's busy again. Asleep naps at his desk; a worker that goes away walks out the door. Desks are real objects: when one is missing, two maintenance guys fetch it from the door, carry it to its spot (the partner brings the chair), and build it, and the new guy waits beside the spot meanwhile. A desk with no worker gets taken apart and hauled out.

Looks come from a seed. pixelGuy hashes it into skin, hair, clothes, and an accessory, and a separate hash stream picks one of 36 races from pixelRaces, so re-rolling the race keeps his clothes. Each guy is drawn into outlined 12×18 frames per pose and facing, and cached.

Your guy

You get the station in the middle with two monitors. Between events you take breaks: wander, chat, or go look over a busy guy's shoulder. officeYou scripts each moment of an ERROR, HACK, or DEFENSE event (your pose, line, main monitor, and shake), and the room's one-word reactions. A tint message recolors your second monitor's graph with a sweep.

Visitors and shows

officeVisitors sends people from other departments in on the frequency setting, up to five at once. Some say a line and go, some stay a minute or two, some settle in. Bosses wear ties. Every guy has a personality from his seed (suck-up, grumbler, deadpan, snark, eager) that shapes how he answers a boss or anyone else. officePerformances plans shows: who performs, what they wear, their lines and crowd cues, the closer, and the snacks. The sim walks performers to the stage with the most open floor in front, gathers the free guys as a crowd, and sends everyone back after the bows.

The world

officeCritters sends a troupe along a clear row from one edge to the other; officeFliers sends a lone flier, a V, a line, or a swarm overhead. Some keep day or night hours, some glow, trail sparks, or cast a shadow. officeDaylight works out the sun's elevation from your clock and a guessed latitude, the moon's real phase, and per scene the sky, stars, and room tint. The webview multiplies the room by a light map that keeps open sky bright and adds pools of light around screens, lamps, and fires.

Talking

officePhrases holds what they say: small talk and scene lines, exchanges the other guy answers, mutters while typing, and musings while idle. officeChatter decides what shows: in tidy, a few bubbles at once by room width, ranked so your lines and visitors go first, and a shown bubble keeps its slot until its line ends. Lines that must be seen always get a bubble. Bubbles are lifted out of each other's way, so they never overlap.

Hooks

Other extensions drive the Office through the API its activate() returns. Without any of them, the room still runs with just your guy.

Being built. The API lands in the next release: setAgents, setMainGuy, playEvent, setTint, playShow, sendVisitor, say, editLook, portraitSvg, and attachView, plus events for clicks, looks, shows, and scene events. Each one will get a live example here.

The webview messages

Under the API, the webview takes these messages, which is also how every demo on this page works:

{ type: "office", scene, agents: [{ id, seed, status: "working" | "idle" | "asleep", title }],
  you: look | null, critters, fliers, dayNight, visitors, performances, chatter, talk, bubbleLook, races, focusedId }
{ type: "sceneEvent", kind: "error" | "hack" | "defense", variant }
{ type: "tint", index, burst }
{ type: "performance", kind }   // a show id, or anything else for a random one

It posts { type: "ready" } when it starts and { type: "open", sessionId } when a guy is clicked.

Contributing

This page is the documentation. A pull request to main that changes what the Office does, a hook, a setting, a command, a scene, a show, or a look must update docs/index.html in the same PR, with a live example where one fits. A check on every PR enforces it; a pure refactor can carry the no-docs-needed label instead.

npm install
npm test            # unit tests
npm run typecheck
npm run site        # build, then copy the bundle and data the site runs into docs/
npx serve docs      # or any static server, then open the page
